UX Case Study / Roommate Coordination

ChoreMate

A roommate chore management concept designed to reduce awkward reminders, assign chores more fairly, and make household effort easier to understand over time.

Role Research synthesis, interaction design, prototyping
Team Sonia Camacho, Daniel De Leon, Pannapat Chanpaisaeng
Focus Shared chores, effort ranking, household transparency

Problem Space

Roommates need a calmer way to coordinate shared chores.

Shared homes often rely on paper lists, calendars, or informal reminders. Those systems can make chores easy to forget and uncomfortable to enforce, especially when roommates are still building trust.

  • Track who completed which chores and when.
  • Rank chores by effort so assignments feel more balanced.
  • Show household progress without requiring awkward reminders.

Research Questions

We focused on what makes chore coordination feel fair.

  • What chores do roommates consider essential in shared spaces?
  • How do roommates divide tasks, and what makes the division feel equal?
  • Are all chores considered equal, or do some feel harder than others?
  • How do roommates communicate that a chore has been completed?

Synthesis

The design opportunity was equal effort, not just equal task count.

The affinity work led us toward a system that could balance chores by perceived difficulty and make progress visible to everyone in the household.

  • Ensure users receive a balanced amount of work.
  • Help users see the effort they are contributing.
  • Make chore assignment feel transparent and comfortable.
